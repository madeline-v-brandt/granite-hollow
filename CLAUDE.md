# climbrec / Granite Hollow

Two things live here:

1. **climbrec**: a Python bouldering recommender (`src/climbrec/`), developed on a simulated crag, Granite Hollow State Park, while we wait to hear about real logbook data. It's paused for now. See README.md.
2. **Granite Hollow, the game** (`site/game/`): a browser game set on that simulated crag. Most current work is here.

## Granite Hollow: the vision

Use this as long-term design guidance when planning or reviewing game features. It isn't a backlog: don't build ideas from it unless asked.

**Granite Hollow is a cozy climbing exploration and strategy game that captures the experience of becoming a rock climber.** Players create a V0 climber (height, ape index, strengths, a weakness) and grow stronger through days spent exploring, climbing, projecting and discovering new areas.

It combines two experiences:

- **A cozy, beautiful world to explore.** Think Stardew Valley's atmosphere and sense of place, centered on outdoor bouldering: forests, trails, distinctive rock, seasons, weather. Spending time there should be a pleasure in itself.
- **Meaningful climbing strategy.** Real decisions about how to spend a climbing day: what to try, when to rest, how to manage energy and skin, explore or project, how to use conditions. Progress should feel earned, and harder grades should take developing skills, not just accumulating XP.

**The goal isn't to simulate grades or optimize XP. It's to capture the feeling of falling in love with climbing, and with a particular climbing place.**

### Design principles

- **Exploration and discovery.** Eventually players won't see every boulder at once. They'll find trails and areas and build their own map and guidebook. Finding a beautiful hidden boulder should be its own reward.
- **Memorable climbs and projects.** Boulders have personalities, names and histories. Working a project over several sessions (learning moves, finding better beta, finally sending) should be one of the most satisfying things in the game.
- **Cozy but strategic.** Relaxing and inviting, never stressful or punishing, but decisions matter. Move away from a clicker loop where you spam Try until you send. Strategy should come out of climbing, exploring, conditions and resources.
- **A living, seasonal world.** Seasons, time of day, weather, temperature, friction and daylight should shape where and how you climb, and areas should feel distinct.
- **Authentic climbing culture.** Quirky names, local legends, obscure classics, funny beta, intimidating projects, supportive partners, the thrill of new rock. Made by people who love climbing, with room for humor and eccentricity.
- **Your own story.** The journal, logbook, CragLog ticks, discovered boulders, memorable sends and long-standing projects together tell the player's climbing story.

### How we build

- Incrementally. Keep the working game working, and make it richer, more beautiful and more engaging step by step.
- Cohesive design over feature count. Avoid unnecessary complexity, micromanagement and tedium.
- Accessible to non-climbers, with enough authentic detail that climbers recognize the experience.

### Directions we may explore later (not commitments)

An illustrated, explorable map with hidden boulders and trails; seasonal changes and time-of-day lighting; move-by-move projecting with beta discovery; climbing styles and techniques that interact with the player's body; recurring NPCs, local characters and climbing partners; a richer personal journal; training and rest choices; hidden areas, legends and special climbs that reward exploration.

## Working on the game

- `site/game/mechanics.js` holds the climbing rules as pure functions (send chance, XP, skills, costs, weather and rain odds, and `canSpot`, which lines you can see). `game.js` is the UI and game state; `illustrate.js` and `paint.js` are the map (below); `world.js` and `knowledge.js` are Phase 2's crag history and what the player knows (below). `index.html` holds the page and CSS tokens (light and dark).
- The look: `site/game/art.js` holds pure functions for the seasons (colours blended by day of year), the light through the day (tint, shadows, phase), and where the scenery goes (trees, flowers, ferns, landmarks, stone walls, brooks). `applyAtmosphere()` in `game.js` writes these as CSS custom properties on the page root, and the map and interface read them, so changing the season or time never redraws the map. Art direction: an illustrated trail map and field guide (warm paper, inked trails, watercolour washes, side-on trees). Fraunces is for headings and map names, Nunito Sans for everything functional (including grades and numbers), and Caveat only for short handwritten notes. Visual changes must not touch `mechanics.js`.
- **The map** is an illustrated park drawn by `site/game/illustrate.js` (pure, testable in Node like `art.js`) and `site/game/paint.js` (the SVG painter). `buildMap()` in `game.js` is a thin adapter: clicks walk you to a boulder or an area, the tooltip shows your sends and the walk, `setMarks()` draws your progress (sent ✓ badges, project rings, locals' tips, your pads; never flags on boulders, which read as a golf course), walking follows the trails' curves, and `applyAtmosphere()` passes the date and clock to `setDate()`/`setTime()`. `illustrate.js` has the palette, boulder forms (`facetForm`: faceted 3D granite, a recipe per kind, from each boulder's description) and `parkScene(D)`, which places the big things once (open ground per area, water, the quarry, the lot, trails, brooks and the millrace, stone walls, ~39k trees with each area's own woods, landmarks from `VIGNETTES`, written from each area's lore) and the small things per 90 m tile through `detail(tx, ty)`; it reuses `Art.scenery`'s streams, walls and ruins. `paint.js` draws the ground always; zoomed out (k < 2.2) a light park view (forest masses, clumped crowns, bare boulder planes; the prototype page only, since the game's world never zooms out past 3.2, `WORLD_MIN_ZOOM`); zoomed in, tiles built the first time they come into view and hidden while off screen. `paper.js` is the paper trail map (M or the MAP button): a printed-looking park map drawn from the same scene, showing the areas the player knows; picking a place moves the camera, never the player. Every climbing day starts at The Tailgate (`START_AREA`). **Walking** (Stage 4, `docs/phase2/stage4-walking.md`): the climber has a position (`G.pos`, metres) and walks with WASD/arrows, a click or a tap; `G.loc` (area and boulder you're at) is derived from it. `travel.js` (pure) plans walks over the trail network: 45 m a game minute on trails (what every area's walk_min was based on) and 30 off them; trips are charged as they go. The painter follows a walking climber with a sliding GPU layer (`setFollow`; the SVG gets a 220 px margin only while you're moving; redrawing the SVG every frame runs at 16–40 fps on a real Mac, see `docs/phase2/stage1-walking.md`), and draws the climber (`setOutfit`, dressed for the weather) above the canopy. Dusk warns you in time and then walks you back to the lot. The game saves itself when the page is hidden, so browser tests must write a save from another page (`game-data.json`) before loading the game. The map keeps its own inks in both themes (a dim veil in dark mode) and its own seasonal palette, set on `#mapwrap`. Its CSS lives in `index.html` under "the map"; class names `field`, `blaze` and `gauge` belong to the interface, so the map uses `hayfield`, `trail-blaze` and `flood-gauge`. Performance: SVG repaints every element on each pan/zoom frame, so merge many small shapes into few paths, cull off-screen tiles, and never fade a group with `opacity` (it composites the layer offscreen each frame; use `fill-opacity` per path), nor use per-element CSS transforms, masks or full-map blend modes. Building the scene takes a second or two when the game starts.
- **Map art prototype** (`site/prototype/park.html`, using the game's `illustrate.js`/`paint.js`): the page where the look was worked out and reviewed, with season, light and weather controls and a list of things to find. Published at https://claude.ai/artifact/BH7gdK6JZkXgoHoGWkSCig with `park-data.json` (the crag without the simulated climbers). `v2/` keeps the approved Hawkwatch-only version and `v1/` the first one. Screenshots: `docs/screenshots/park-prototype/`, `docs/screenshots/game-illustrated-map/`, and earlier rounds in `hawkwatch-prototype*/` and `hawkwatch-boulders-*/`.
- **Phase 2: a crag developed as you play** (plan and decisions: `docs/phase2/stage0-design.md`; done: Stage 0 the foundations, Stage 1 the walking benchmark (`docs/phase2/stage1-walking.md`), Stage 2 the trail map and zoom limits (`docs/phase2/stage2-paper-map.md`), Stage 3 finding boulders, spotting lines and first ascents (`docs/phase2/stage3-discovery.md`), Stage 4 the climber and walking (`docs/phase2/stage4-walking.md`)). **The new rules are live:** new climbers start under them, and old ones are converted on load. The park reopened to climbing in September 2025; each save has its own Granite Hollow, developed by its own NPC community alongside the player, who finds boulders, spots lines and makes first ascents. Four layers, kept apart: the roster (`game-data.json`, never changes in play), the history (`G.world`, written only by `world.js`: the append-only first-ascent ledger `est`, arrived NPCs, boulders the community has found, notable `news`; simulated one day at a time from a seeded stream, so reloading never rerolls a day), what the player knows (`G.know`, written only by `knowledge.js`: areas and boulders unknown/known/visited, identified lines), and the player's own climbing (`G.ps`, `G.ticks`). **Every screen gets names, grades and lists from `K` (knowledge.js), never from `D` or `G.world` directly**, or undiscovered things leak (map labels and boulders' accessible names go through `P.setKnown`). A v1 save (the old rules) runs K in "everything known" mode; it now only exists in development mode (the dev panel's "old rules" switch), which is how the old screens are regression-checked. `World.migrate` turns a v1 save into v2 deterministically (sends become your FAs, tried problems become identified lines); `game.js` does it on load (on a copy, keeping the old save under `granite-hollow-save-before-reopening`, and falling back to the old rules if it fails) and for loaded v1 save files, then shows a welcome back. Inspecting a boulder (`K.inspect`) records it in `G.know.looked`; your own FA's name is the only thing the ledger lets you change (`World.nameFa`). Dialogs go through `openModal` (a queue: FA naming, then level-up, then the day summary). Pacing lives in `World.PARAMS`. Development mode: `?dev` (its own save slot, dev tools, a switch to start climbers under the old rules, never the leaderboard; `?dev=0` leaves; its save files are refused outside it). Leaderboard entries carry `rules` (the rules a climber started under) so old and new progress aren't compared. Tests: `node scripts/test_world.js` (add `--curve` to see how the crag develops), and in headless Chrome `scripts/browser/` (`v2test.js` is the leak test, rerun it after every stage; `live.js` is normal mode: a new climber, converting an old one, save files; `walk.js` is walking, trips, dusk and the frame rate).
- After changing pacing or rules, run `node scripts/balance_game.js` (200 simulated players). Current pacing: about V3 by climbing day 5, V5 by 12, V7 by 22, V9 by 39.
- `game-data.json` is generated: `.venv/bin/python -m climbrec.viz.game_data`. The map page's data comes from `python -m climbrec.viz.crag_map`.
- **Don't break players' saves.** Saves (localStorage and downloaded save files) and the shared leaderboard refer to problems by index and to boulders by position. Reordering, adding or removing problems or boulders isn't safe unless you migrate saves. Renaming problems and boulders is safe, but saves store **sector names** (`G.loc.sector`, `G.visited`): if you rename a sector, add the old name to `OLD_SECTOR_NAMES` in `game.js` (`migrateSave` translates it on load). Keep old save fields readable (see `validateSave`, `migrateSave` and the fallbacks for older forecasts and ticks), and test changes with an old, mid-day save, not just a fresh game (`scripts/browser/fixtures/v1-midday.json`). Saves are `v: 1` (old rules, converted to v2 on load) or `v: 2` (Phase 2, with `world`, `know` and `rules`); `World.validate` checks a v2 save's new parts, and new fields must be optional there (older v2 saves won't have them). First-ascent records are permanent: never rewrite `by` or `date`.
- Names, boulder themes, boulder descriptions and the crag's lore live in `src/climbrec/simulate/naming.py`. Keep new names and text consistent with that lore (the quarry and mill village, the drowned valley, Dot, the Bishop, Ranger Gus, Pickles, and the 1987 closure and 2025 reopening; the game's side, with the notebook page's four problems and the local legends, is in `world.js`). The `sector_order`/`boulder_order` columns keep the map in place when names change.
- The game's Mountain-Project-like site is called **CragLog**. Never put a real company's name or branding on a published page.
- The game is published as a claude.ai artifact at https://claude.ai/artifact/52JmdfSGKsApoYMAGPycnS (the crag map: https://claude.ai/artifact/Vn4mQD8u8AvbHuShnY1baD). The game uses the artifact's `db` (leaderboard, `runs/{self}`), `user` and `downloads` capabilities. Republish with the same file path. Data shared through the leaderboard is untrusted: validate it and escape it.
- **GitHub:** https://github.com/madeline-v-brandt/granite-hollow (public). `.github/workflows/pages.yml` publishes `site/` to GitHub Pages on every push to `main`: https://madeline-v-brandt.github.io/granite-hollow/ (the root redirects to `game/`; also `map/` and `prototype/park.html`). Off claude.ai the leaderboard is off and saves are separate (move them with save files). The git folder is `.git.nosync` (outside iCloud; `.git` points to it). Commits use the GitHub noreply address. `inspo/` and `8a.txt` stay local (gitignored). Push only when asked.

## Working on the recommender

- Python env: `.venv.nosync`, with `.venv` linked to it. The folder syncs to iCloud, which hides `.pth` files inside a synced `.venv`, and Python 3.13+ skips hidden `.pth` files. Use `.venv/bin/python`.
- Tests: `.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q`. Regenerate the crag: `.venv/bin/python -m climbrec.simulate`. It's deterministic, and naming uses its own random stream, so names can change without changing grades, styles or ticks.
- Real data: don't scrape sites whose terms forbid it (Mountain Project's do). We've asked Mountain Project for permission and are waiting to hear back.
