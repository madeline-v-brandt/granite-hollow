# climbrec / Granite Hollow

Two things live here:

1. **climbrec**: a Python bouldering recommender (`src/climbrec/`), developed on a simulated crag, Granite Hollow State Park, while we wait to hear about real logbook data. It's paused for now. See README.md.
2. **Granite Hollow, the game** (`site/game/`): a browser game set on that simulated crag. Most current work is here.

## Granite Hollow: the vision

Use this as long-term design guidance when planning or reviewing game features. It isn't a backlog: don't build ideas from it unless asked.

**Granite Hollow is a cozy climbing exploration and strategy game that captures the experience of becoming a rock climber.** Players create a V0 climber (height, ape index, strengths, a weakness) and grow stronger through days spent exploring, climbing, projecting and discovering new areas.

It combines two experiences:

- **A cozy, beautiful world to explore.** Think Stardew Valley's atmosphere and sense of place, centered on outdoor bouldering: forests, trails, distinctive rock, seasons, weather. Spending time there should be a pleasure in itself.
- **Meaningful climbing strategy.** Real decisions about how to spend a climbing day: what to try, when to rest, how to manage energy and skin, explore or project, how to use conditions. Progress should feel earned, and harder grades should take developing skills, not just accumulating XP.

**The goal isn't to simulate grades or optimize XP. It's to capture the feeling of falling in love with climbing, and with a particular climbing place.**

### Design principles

- **Exploration and discovery.** Eventually players won't see every boulder at once. They'll find trails and areas and build their own map and guidebook. Finding a beautiful hidden boulder should be its own reward.
- **Memorable climbs and projects.** Boulders have personalities, names and histories. Working a project over several sessions (learning moves, finding better beta, finally sending) should be one of the most satisfying things in the game.
- **Cozy but strategic.** Relaxing and inviting, never stressful or punishing, but decisions matter. Move away from a clicker loop where you spam Try until you send. Strategy should come out of climbing, exploring, conditions and resources.
- **A living, seasonal world.** Seasons, time of day, weather, temperature, friction and daylight should shape where and how you climb, and areas should feel distinct.
- **Authentic climbing culture.** Quirky names, local legends, obscure classics, funny beta, intimidating projects, supportive partners, the thrill of new rock. Made by people who love climbing, with room for humor and eccentricity.
- **Your own story.** The journal, logbook, CragLog ticks, discovered boulders, memorable sends and long-standing projects together tell the player's climbing story.

### How we build

- Incrementally. Keep the working game working, and make it richer, more beautiful and more engaging step by step.
- Cohesive design over feature count. Avoid unnecessary complexity, micromanagement and tedium.
- Accessible to non-climbers, with enough authentic detail that climbers recognize the experience.

### Directions we may explore later (not commitments)

An illustrated, explorable map with hidden boulders and trails; seasonal changes and time-of-day lighting; move-by-move projecting with beta discovery; climbing styles and techniques that interact with the player's body; recurring NPCs, local characters and climbing partners; a richer personal journal; training and rest choices; hidden areas, legends and special climbs that reward exploration.

## Working on the game

- `site/game/mechanics.js` holds the climbing rules as pure functions (send chance, XP, skills, costs, weather and rain odds, and `canSpot`, which lines you can see). Since Phase 3's Stage 0b, every balance number is in its `BAL` object (level costs, XP by grade, the send curve, the shared ceiling on bonuses, learning and rests). Use `Mech.levelOf(player)` and `Mech.ability(player)`: a level also needs proof (`player.tops`, first sends by grade, recounted from the ticks on load), so bare XP isn't enough. Since Stage 1, every problem has a sequence of moves (`Mech.sequence(p)`, generated from the problem's index and data, never stored; `p.seq` in the game), with one or two cruxes and sometimes a better way through a crux (`alt`: a heel hook, a drop-knee, an intermediate for shorter people, a hold taller people can skip). A burn's send chance is unchanged; the moves decide where you fall. What you know is kept per move (`ps.mk`, and `ps.alt` once found), and `ps.beta` (what the send chance reads) is worked out from it, so saves from before moves keep their beta. Tried problems show a move strip (`moveStrip` in `game.js`). `game.js` is the UI and game state; `illustrate.js` and `paint.js` are the map (below); `world.js` and `knowledge.js` are Phase 2's crag history and what the player knows (below); `social.js` is Phase 4's record of you and the regulars; `talk.js` and `portraits.js` are what they say and how they look. `index.html` holds the page and CSS tokens (light and dark).
- The look: `site/game/art.js` holds pure functions for the seasons (colours blended by day of year), the light through the day (tint, shadows, phase), and where the scenery goes (trees, flowers, ferns, landmarks, stone walls, brooks). `applyAtmosphere()` in `game.js` writes these as CSS custom properties on the page root, and the map and interface read them, so changing the season or time never redraws the map. Art direction: an illustrated trail map and field guide (warm paper, inked trails, watercolour washes, side-on trees). Fraunces is for headings and map names, Nunito Sans for everything functional (including grades and numbers), and Caveat only for short handwritten notes. Visual changes must not touch `mechanics.js`.
- **The map** is an illustrated park drawn by `site/game/illustrate.js` (pure, testable in Node like `art.js`) and `site/game/paint.js` (the SVG painter). `buildMap()` in `game.js` is a thin adapter: clicks walk you to a boulder or an area, the tooltip shows your sends and the walk, `setMarks()` draws your progress (sent ✓ badges, project rings, locals' tips, your pads; never flags on boulders, which read as a golf course), walking follows the trails' curves, and `applyAtmosphere()` passes the date and clock to `setDate()`/`setTime()`. `illustrate.js` has the palette, boulder forms (`facetForm`: faceted 3D granite, a recipe per kind, from each boulder's description) and `parkScene(D)`, which places the big things once (open ground per area, water, the quarry, the lot, trails, brooks and the millrace, stone walls, ~39k trees with each area's own woods, landmarks from `VIGNETTES`, written from each area's lore) and the small things per 90 m tile through `detail(tx, ty)`; it reuses `Art.scenery`'s streams, walls and ruins. `paint.js` draws the ground always; zoomed out (k < 2.2) a light park view (forest masses, clumped crowns, bare boulder planes; the prototype page only, since the game's world never zooms out past 3.2, `WORLD_MIN_ZOOM`); zoomed in, tiles built the first time they come into view and hidden while off screen. `paper.js` is the paper trail map (M or the MAP button; the landing page lies over a static export of it, `paper-map.svg`, made by `scripts/browser/export_paper_map.js`): a printed-looking park map drawn from the same scene, showing the areas the player knows; picking a place moves the camera, never the player. Every climbing day starts at The Tailgate (`START_AREA`). **Walking** (Stage 4, `docs/phase2/stage4-walking.md`): the climber has a position (`G.pos`, metres) and walks with WASD/arrows, a click or a tap; `G.loc` (area and boulder you're at) is derived from it. `travel.js` (pure) plans walks over the trail network. Trips the game walks for you (Go there, the trail map's Walk) keep to the trails at 45 m a game minute on trails (what every area's walk_min was based on) and 30 off them; walking yourself (keys, a tap on the map) is a little quicker (`Travel.MANUAL`: 50 and 35); the keys cut through the woods, so knowing the shortcuts pays, but a tap on anything past `Travel.DIRECT_OK` (110 m) keeps to the trails. Arriving at an area goes to the nearest open spot to its centre (`freeSpot`; The Schoolhouse's centre is inside The Blackboard), and a save found inside a boulder is moved out on load. WASD and the arrows walk whatever has focus, except a text field. Trips are charged as they go. The climber faces four ways (l, r, u away, d towards you). The painter follows a walking climber with a sliding GPU layer (`setFollow`; the SVG gets a 220 px margin only while you're moving; redrawing the SVG every frame runs at 16–40 fps on a real Mac, see `docs/phase2/stage1-walking.md`), and draws the climber (`setOutfit`, dressed for the weather) in a small SVG of their own layered over the map (`.me-layer`; anything that changes every frame inside the map SVG makes the browser repaint the whole park every frame: Stage 6). Every day starts in the lot by the trailhead end (`PARK_SPOT`; your car is two spaces along, so you don't walk into it: `art.js`; the lot is a rectangle west of The Billboard, with a straight path from its north-east corner to the Tailgate's trail junction (`LOT_PATH`)); parked cars are solid. `game-data.json` and `paper-map.svg` are fetched with the page's version (`ASSET_V`), so bumping `?v=` in `index.html` refreshes them too. Boulders and trees in front of you are copied over you in a small layer sized to them (`placeOccluders`), with a faint outline of you on top. The regulars stand at boulders each day (`peopleToday` in `game.js`, `setPeople`/`personAt` in `paint.js`); walk up to someone to talk; they're drawn your size at every zoom (`sizePeople`). Boulders' drawn heights run from the climber's height to 4× it (`drawnHeight` in `illustrate.js`); shapes come in families (`FAMILY`), and trees still use the old heights (`H0`). Each boulder's hidden sides are drawn first in shade (`back`), so overhangs never float. The lakes are lobed (`lakeShape`, fitted round the boulders, trails and brooks) and drawn with shallows, the sky, reeds and the far shore's trees reflected in the season's colours. Dusk warns you in time and then walks you back to the lot. The game saves itself when the page is hidden, so browser tests must write a save from another page (`game-data.json`) before loading the game. The map keeps its own inks in both themes (a dim veil in dark mode) and its own seasonal palette, set on `#mapwrap`. Its CSS lives in `index.html` under "the map"; class names `field`, `blaze` and `gauge` belong to the interface, so the map uses `hayfield`, `trail-blaze` and `flood-gauge`. Performance: SVG repaints every element on each pan/zoom frame, so merge many small shapes into few paths, cull off-screen tiles, and never fade a group with `opacity` (it composites the layer offscreen each frame; use `fill-opacity` per path), nor use per-element CSS transforms, masks or full-map blend modes. Building the scene takes a second or two when the game starts.

- After changing pacing or rules, run `node scripts/balance_game.js` (four playstyles: steady, projector, mileage and explorer; 40 simulated climbers each; a few minutes; `--set '{"levelGrowth":1.3}'` tries other `BAL` numbers, `--rules` a whole candidate rules file, `--rates` the days spent at each level). The climbers play in a crag that develops around them and only climb lines they've found. Current pacing (Stage 5's final balance; medians of 16): steady climbers are on the agreed pace (V3 day 5, V5 15, V7 27, V9 54, V11 99, V13 about 171); projectors reach V13 about day 149 (142 if they work moves); mileage and explorer climbers about day 118; climbing alone keeps to the targets (`docs/phase3/stage5-balance.md`). - **Play-test rules (2026-11-26):**
  - Rests give back a full 12 energy every time (`BAL.restDay` = 1).
  - The community makes no first ascents before September 7 (`World.PARAMS.faFrom`).
  - A new climber's first day is at least Good (`Mech.atLeast`). Its targets are levels, not hardest sends: V3 about climbing day 6, V5 15, V7 30, V9 55, V11 100, V13 180+. Saves carry `bal: 3`; older ones are regraded once on load (`regrade` in `game.js`: level recomputed by replaying the ticks, never lowered; skills made relative; a copy kept under `granite-hollow-save-before-regrade`). Climbers who started under this balance carry `balStart: 3` and post to leaderboard rules 3.
- `game-data.json` is generated: `PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python -m climbrec.viz.game_data` (without `PYTHONPATH` the package isn't found). It's deterministic. Boulders placed by hand (off the lot and paths) are in `MOVED` in `simulate/layout.py`. The map page's data comes from `python -m climbrec.viz.crag_map`.
- **Don't break players' saves.** Saves (localStorage and downloaded save files) and the shared leaderboard refer to problems by index and to boulders by position. Reordering, adding or removing problems or boulders isn't safe unless you migrate saves. Renaming problems and boulders is safe, but saves store **sector names** (`G.loc.sector`, `G.visited`): if you rename a sector, add the old name to `OLD_SECTOR_NAMES` in `game.js` (`migrateSave` translates it on load). Keep old save fields readable (see `validateSave`, `migrateSave` and the fallbacks for older forecasts and ticks), and test changes with an old, mid-day save, not just a fresh game (`scripts/browser/fixtures/v1-midday.json`). Saves are `v: 1` (old rules, converted to v2 on load) or `v: 2` (Phase 2, with `world`, `know` and `rules`); `World.validate` checks a v2 save's new parts, and new fields must be optional there (older v2 saves won't have them). First-ascent records are permanent: never rewrite `by` or `date`.
- Names, boulder themes, boulder descriptions and the crag's lore live in `src/climbrec/simulate/naming.py`. Keep new names and text consistent with that lore (the quarry and mill village, the drowned valley, Dot, the Bishop, Ranger Gus, Pickles, and the 1987 closure and 2025 reopening; the game's side, with the notebook page's four problems and the local legends, is in `world.js`). The `sector_order`/`boulder_order` columns keep the map in place when names change.
